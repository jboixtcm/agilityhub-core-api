## Steps
1. **The checkout of a pending readmission uses the applicant's address (review #1, R-04-06 c, R-04-26).** `CheckoutService.create()` sends the LEFT record's primary email to the payment provider as the customer. Use the submitted primary address while the readmission is pending. The account's login email is never touched. Extend T-04-12 or T-04-22: `FakeCheckoutGateway` captures the customer email, and it is the submitted address.
2. **Recipient caps are admitted once per notification (review #2, R-04-20).** Today every delivery attempt charges the cap, retries included. With a cap of 1, a failure after the admission makes the retry skip the send and succeed, and no mail ever leaves.
   - Key the admission by the event (and the notification), so that a retry of the same event reuses its admission and sends.
   - Only new events count against the cap.
   - Test: cap 1, and the first attempt fails after the admission (the mail sender throws once). The retry sends, and the consumer is marked processed only after the send.
3. **N-01 and N-03 take their values from the event (review #3, R-04-06 c, S04 §8).** The locale, the dog names, the plan and the upfront total of a submission come from its event payload, never from the dog blocks, which a later submission can change.
   - Extend the payload where a value is missing, and keep `CATALEG_ESDEVENIMENTS.md` in step (propose the change in the report).
   - Test: readmission A is rejected, then readmission B reuses the same dog, both before dispatch. A's N-03 carries A's locale and values, and B's N-01 carries B's.
4. **A generation-aware signup configuration cache (review #4, R-04-27).** A load that started before an eviction must never store its old value; use the existing `CacheLoads`. Deterministic test: a load is paused across the commit and eviction of a parameter write, then resumed; the next `GET /signup` is fresh.
5. **The first month in the D2 signup view (web E3-W07, R-04-15, S04 §3).** `GET /members/{id}/signup` and the D2 dry run return `upfront.firstMonth {option, portion, startDate, amountDue}` for a submission with a `FIRST_MONTH` line (absent otherwise). The values are the ones frozen at submission, and the ones recalculated by the dry run. With it, D2 names the month and «(mitja quota)» without reimplementing the rule. The web's `pending.json` has the proposed shape. Contract test, plus an IT for a monthly plan submitted on day 5 (`TODAY`/`FULL`) and on day 17 (`TODAY`/`HALF`).

#### Assumptions (round 2)
1. **Point 1, where the decision lives.** In a new club-scoped collection, not on the outbox record. The dispatcher's transaction has already locked the event document (`records.lock`), and `deliver` runs outside that transaction (`NOT_SUPPORTED`). A write next to `processedAt` would therefore wait on that lock. The new collection is the consumer's own, and it holds no recipient data.
2. **Point 1, the retention.** 1 day. This is a fixed infrastructure value, like the outbox's own settings, not a club parameter. The outbox retries at most 10 times, at most 300 s apart, plus five-minute claim leases, so every retry of an event finds its decision; after a day the TTL removes it. A restart still empties the per-instance buckets («per-instance token buckets for the E0 single-instance deployment»). After a restart, new events count against a fresh bucket, as the organizer's test describes.
3. **Point 1, a refusal is stored too.** An event capped once stays capped on its retries (for example when its admin copies fail later), as in round 1.
4. **Point 2, the rule.** Only the frozen amounts decide, as the point asks. The frozen `amountDue` is compared with the plan's `MONTHLY_FEE` price on the submission day (`signup.submittedAt` in the club's time zone): the full price, or its half rounded half-up like the calculator. The portion is `null` when the amounts cannot tell:
   - no such plan or price that day;
   - another currency;
   - an amount that is neither;
   - a price whose half equals it (0 or 1 minor unit).

   A start-date rule for `ALTERNATIVE` would also be exact, but I did not keep it, so that one rule serves both options.
5. **Point 2, the contract.** `portion` stays required and becomes nullable. This widens a response field, so the change is not purely additive; the organizer's point allows `null`. A block frozen since E3-T12 always stores its portion, so only older blocks can give `null`.
6. **Point 3.** Whether `firstMonth` is present depends on the lines of that answer: `payments.lines(...)` in the view; in the dry run, the current lines, or the replacement lines after a plan change. The value itself is unchanged: the frozen month in the view, the month in force in the dry run.
