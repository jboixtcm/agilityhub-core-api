### Assumptions
1. **P5 did not exist as a `Job` bean.** The "Context to load" names "the P5 job (`expirations`) and its steps", but P5 had only
   its catalog row: no class implemented it, and `JobCatalogContractTest` listed it among the bean-less rows. So this task
   registers P5 with step h only; steps a–g stay with their verticals (S15 §9: E8, E9). What changes because of that:
   - The fixed convention holds: the catalog stays at ten processes, P5 has no module and P7 keeps `SINGLE_CLASS`.
   - `GET /jobs` (D11) now lists `expirations` for every club, as R-15-01 expects of a process with a bean. The web's D11
     tests or fixtures that pin the exact list of rows may need the new row.
   - The scheduler now runs P5 once a day in every club. With no lapsed session, a run is `SUCCEEDED` with no item.
2. **Home of P5.** P5 lives in `clubs.common`, next to P1 and P9, and reaches other contexts through its own ports
   (`clubs.common` → `payments.application`, which the architecture rules allow, and no cycle). A step SPI in `payments` would
   have made `payments` → `clubs.common` → `clubs.census` → `payments`, a cycle.
3. **Cut-off.** Step h's cut-off is the occurrence (`scheduledFor`), as in P7 and P9, so the plan and the effects see the same
   sessions. A session that lapses after 06:00 waits for the next day's run, or for the next write that needs its rows
   (`releaseLapsed`). Reads show its rows payable meanwhile. The smoke therefore moves the server clock to the first P5
   occurrence after `expiresAt`.
4. **The fake has no network client.** It "gets the timeout" by declaring `callTimeout() = MAX_CALL_TIMEOUT`. The bound is a
   code constant (30 s), as the SendGrid and Twilio clients' timeouts are, not a configuration property. E8's Stripe adapter
   must declare its client's timeout, and the startup check refuses one above 30 s.
5. **#5 goes beyond `held`.** `abandon` is guarded too. In the review's scenario (Mongo unreachable), guarding only `held` would
   still let `abandon`'s failure hide the original one. When `abandon` fails, the claim stays until its lease, as for a stopped
   process (E79).
