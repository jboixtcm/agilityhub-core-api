# Review E8-T09 — needs changes

Round 5 fixes and tests the three round-4 findings: the CREDIT top-up, a failed compensation becoming terminal, and reconciliation committing while Stripe is disabled. The counterfactual (`81`) fails 7 of 7 on the reviewed code. The generator is a real improvement: it found a fourth defect, an admin refund accepted after a full CREDIT. The ledger itself looks correct. The problems below are about *when* the new "intervention" state is reached and what happens after it.

## Findings

1. **major** `src/main/java/com/agilityhub/core/payments/application/PaymentRefunds.java:117` and `:208-214` — **A short configuration or rate-limit outage permanently turns automatic cancellation refunds into manual work that nobody is told about.**
   - **What happens:** when a compensation command is rejected before reaching Stripe (401/403, `PROVIDER_CONFIG_INVALID`, all-429) and runs out of retries, the exhausted callback sets `providerStatus=failed`. `refundFailed()` then reads that exactly like a refund the bank refused, so `compensateRemaining` calls `intervene()`.
   - **How fast:** the retry budget is 3 attempts with 10 s then 20 s backoff (`PaymentRetryState.java:22`), and recovery runs every 10 s. So within about a minute of a revoked or rotated key, every REFUND-policy cancellation becomes a permanent intervention. No money moved and no Stripe refund object exists.
   - **Inconsistency:** a *disabled* provider defers without spending attempts (`:193-197`), but a *misconfigured* provider becomes terminal.
   - **Nobody is told:** an intervention is only `compensationInterventionAt`, which no API exposes, plus one WARN line, which labels the payment id as `eventId=` (`PaymentRetryPolicy.java:75`). The club admin has no way to learn that a member is owed D, or how much.
   - **Test change:** `CardPaymentsIT` round-4 point-2 (around line 1416) was rewritten so that repairing the configuration no longer resumes the compensation. Rounds 1–3 kept asking that a configuration error "release or retry" the refund (R-12-20 automatic REFUND).
   - **Suggested fix:** keep terminal-on-failure only for refunds that Stripe reports as `failed`/`canceled` (webhook). Treat an exhausted pre-submission rejection of a compensation command like the disabled path (defer), or let it count toward the bound without being terminal.
   - **Organizer decision needed** (the executor raised it, correctly, under the closed catalogs): is a log-only intervention acceptable, or is an admin-visible signal needed?

2. **minor** `PaymentRefunds.java:117` (`commands.size() >= retries.maxAttempts()`) — **`billing.stripeMaxAttempts` is reused with a different meaning, and the cap turns normal cases into interventions.**
   - In the catalog and R-12-18 this parameter means card retries per invoice. Here it caps how many compensation *commands* a payment may have.
   - Supplements are already bounded without it: each one exists only because a distinct non-compensation refund failed, and failed compensations are now terminal.
   - Example (`CapturedPaymentInvariantsIT.java:162-180`): four failed admin partial refunds leave 2 € as a manual intervention, even though no compensation failed.
   - Fix: drop the cap and rely on terminal-on-failure, or have the organizer rule on the new meaning.

3. **minor** `PaymentRefunds.java:319-327` (`settled()`, upfront branch) — **A refund made directly in Stripe after a CREDIT is not reconciled with the credit.**
   - The allocation `min(remaining, captured − refunded)` ignores K.
   - Example: a 12 € credit, still unbilled, plus a 12 € dashboard refund. The payment becomes `REFUNDED`, the credit stays −12 € and gets billed. The member receives 24 €, the ledger is inconsistent, and nothing is logged.
   - `due()` silently clamps to 0, and no production code calls `CapturedPaymentLedger.consistent()`.
   - The generator only creates refunds through the fake provider, and only for one payment per intent, so it cannot reach this case (task point 6 covers dashboard refunds).
   - Fix: shrink an unbilled credit by the overshoot (with a conditional update); if the credit is already billed, record an intervention or WARN. Add dashboard refunds to the generator.
